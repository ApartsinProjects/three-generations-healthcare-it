# Fable AIIM Position-Paper Review (R1)
## "Computable Clinical Intent: Recovering Executable Care Actions from Clinical Communication"

Reviewer: simulated top-tier AIIM Position Paper reviewer (Fable model), line-by-line pass on index.html.

## 1. Recommendation

**Major revision.**

The problem is real, well-motivated, and squarely within AIIM's remit, and the paper is unusually clearly written. But in its current form it is a well-argued framing document rather than a position paper that "gathers, describes, and analyzes the scientific challenges" of a field. The body runs to roughly 3,300 words (including tables, captions, and the worked example); the section that actually analyzes the scientific challenges (5.3) is two paragraphs, giving each of six open sub-problems a single sentence. The central novelty claim (that the ACR is distinct from existing action-item/order-extraction work and from FHIR workflow resources) is asserted rather than demonstrated, and on the FHIR side it is vulnerable: FHIR already has `ServiceRequest.intent` (proposal / plan / order), `asNeeded[x]` for conditional requests, `Task.restriction.period` for due windows, and `Provenance`, which is close to the authors' own stated falsification condition for the ACR. The evaluation framework is a list of metric names with no definitions, no alignment procedure, and no definition of "operationally unsafe". The only empirical support is an unreviewed self-cited arXiv preprint on a synthetic benchmark, reported without a single number. The worked example contains a clinical-realism error (a discharge summary "authored by the PCP"). None of these is fatal; all are fixable; together they mean the manuscript needs substantial expansion and sharpening before it delivers what the article type promises.

## 2. Summary of contribution (as claimed)

The authors propose organizing health IT into cumulative computational layers by the "unit of computability": the record, the discrete clinical fact, and, proposed here, patient-specific clinical intent. They define the Actionable Clinical Record (ACR), a nine-attribute tuple representing a source-grounded, temporally normalized, executable intended action recovered from unstructured clinical communication, positioned upstream of FHIR Task/ServiceRequest/CarePlan. They argue that recovering ACRs is a distinct AI-in-medicine problem with six open sub-problems, that it requires a hybrid neuro-symbolic architecture governed by output invariants, and that it should be evaluated on "executable correctness" rather than text overlap. A worked example and a companion feasibility study (ref 51) are offered as evidence of tractability, together with a research agenda and falsifiable predictions.

## 3. Strengths

- **S1 (Sec. 1, Sec. 4).** The core observation is correct and important: interoperability standards represent intent only after someone has structured it, and the capture step from communication is under-served. The prescribed / observed / intended distinction (Table 2) is a genuinely useful way to locate the gap relative to CIGs and process mining.
- **S2 (Sec. 5.1, worked example).** Making the abstraction concrete with a fully populated ACR pair and a mapping to Task/ServiceRequest is the right move for a position paper, and the "What the layers below cannot supply" row states the delta crisply.
- **S3 (Sec. 5.1).** Separating a representational readiness axis (mentioned -> interpreted -> actionable -> executable) from an operational status axis is a clean idea that prevents a common conflation in the follow-up-extraction literature.
- **S4 (Sec. 6).** Framing auditability, deterministic temporal computation, and abstention as properties of the *output contract* rather than of model capability is exactly the right stance for a safety-relevant AI application, and it will age well as generators improve.
- **S5 (Sec. 8, last paragraph).** Stating two explicit conditions under which the paper's own constructs would be redundant is rare in position papers and to be commended, even though (see W2) the first condition is closer to being met than the authors acknowledge.
- **S6.** Writing is disciplined and free of hype; the reference list is current (2025-2026), Vancouver-formatted, and largely well chosen.

## 4. Major weaknesses (severity order)

**W1. The challenge analysis is far too thin for the article type.** AIIM defines a position paper as one that "gather[s], describe[s], and analyze[s] the scientific challenges of a specific field, founding them on the related literature." Section 5.3, the only section that does this, devotes one sentence to each of six sub-problems: "*Temporal normalization* must turn 'in two weeks' or 'before the next visit' into a resolvable constraint, a setting where general-purpose language models are known to be weak [41]." That is a statement of the problem, not an analysis. For each sub-problem an AIIM reader expects: what the state of the art is (with results), what specifically fails and why, what makes the clinical-intent instance harder than the general case, and what would count as solved. Example gaps: for temporal normalization, THYME/Clinical TempEval already normalize to TIMEX3 values, so the open problem is not normalization but *anchor selection* ("in two weeks" from what: discharge date, dictation date, next visit?) and *unresolvable relative constraints* ("before the next visit" when no visit is scheduled); the paper never names these. For actor disambiguation, the single cited work [49] is speaker-role identification, which is precisely *not* the accountable-performer problem the authors say they need. For cross-message reconciliation and conditional/dependency logic there are zero citations. Fix: expand 5.3 into a full section (roughly 1,500-2,500 words) with one subsection per sub-problem, each grounded in 3-6 references and ending with a concrete "solved-when" criterion that ties back to the evaluation framework.

**W2. The differentiation from FHIR is under-argued and possibly wrong on its own terms.** Section 8 states the ACR is redundant "if direct mapping into existing workflow standards, with generic recovery metadata, captures the required action, actor, temporal, conditional, dependency, provenance, and uncertainty semantics." A FHIR-literate reviewer will note that R5 already provides: `ServiceRequest.intent` with the RequestIntent value set (proposal | plan | directive | order | ...), which is literally an "intent" attribute; `ServiceRequest.asNeeded[x]` for condition-gated requests (the "if palpitations recur" case); `occurrence[x]` with `Timing` for relative and recurring constraints; `Task.restriction.period` for due windows; `basedOn` / `partOf` / `replaces` for dependency and reconciliation; `status = draft` for unconfirmed recoveries; the `Provenance` resource; and extensions for confidence. The paper's own Section 5.2 concedes that ACR-to-FHIR translation is "profile-specific" and that FHIR semantics "differ from the ACR's" without saying how. Nor does the manuscript ever mention `ServiceRequest.intent`, which is a conspicuous omission given the title. Fix: add a table mapping every ACR attribute to the candidate FHIR R5 element(s), and for each row state either "lossless" or exactly what is lost (my guess at the defensible residue: per-attribute calibrated confidence, the readiness rung, *unresolved* anchors that FHIR Timing cannot hold, and the extracted-vs-inferred distinction). If that residue is the real contribution, say so explicitly and drop the broader "distinct object" rhetoric.

**W3. The differentiation from existing clinical NLP is asserted, not demonstrated.** Section 5.3: "Even order extraction from conversation targets a flat schema scored by text overlap [29]; the distinction the ACR draws is operational, requiring that a recovered action be source-grounded, temporally normalized, and carry the actor, condition, and dependency structure." MEDIQA-OE orders carry order type, description, reason, and *provenance* (utterance IDs), and provenance is scored, so "source-grounded" is not new there. CLIP [37] has typed action items (Appointment, Lab, Procedure, Medication, Imaging, Patient instructions, Other). MedDec [38] extracts decision categories with spans. n2c2 2018 [34] and the i2b2 2012 temporal challenge [45] give event-attribute-time structures. Each ACR attribute therefore has a precedent; the claim must be that the *conjunction*, under an *execution* target, changes the problem. That claim can be made, but it needs a feature-by-feature comparison table (CLIP, MedDec, MEDIQA-OE, radiology-recommendation extraction [32], n2c2/i2b2 temporal tasks, ACR) across: action typing, target coding, actor/performer, normalized time, condition, dependency, provenance, confidence, execution target. Without it, an NLP reader will conclude the ACR is CLIP plus HeidelTime. Also missing entirely: the task-oriented-dialogue NLU tradition in which "intent + slots" is a thirty-year-old frame (the ACR *is* an intent frame), and executable semantic parsing (text-to-SQL, execution accuracy), which is the direct antecedent of "executable correctness." For an AI journal these are the most relevant lineages and their absence is striking.

**W4. The evaluation framework is not specified enough to be used, which undercuts "This framework, not any single score, is the intended contribution" (Sec. 7).** The framework is a comma-separated list of metric names. Unanswered: (i) how predicted ACRs are aligned to gold ACRs before any record-level metric can be computed (the number of records will differ; is alignment on action+target? on span overlap? Hungarian matching?); (ii) what "whole-record exact match" means when optional attributes are unpopulated in gold; (iii) the unit and tolerance of "temporal-normalization error" (days? and for recurring or event-anchored constraints?); (iv) how "unsupported-action rate" is grounded (against source spans? against gold?); (v) what the selective-risk curve is over, and what coverage targets are meaningful; (vi) whether errors are consequence-weighted, given Section 6 says higher-consequence actions get tighter review; (vii) what "operationally unsafe output" means, which is needed for the framework's own falsification test in Section 8; (viii) how gold is created when actor or timing is not stated (annotator inference rules, expected agreement). Fix: a table of metrics with formal definitions, a stated alignment procedure, and a worked scoring of the paper's own example against a perturbed prediction (e.g., +7 d instead of +14 d; actor = cardiology instead of PCP) showing which metrics fire.

**W5. The evidence of tractability is a single self-cited, unreviewed preprint on a synthetic benchmark, reported without numbers.** Section 7: "the hybrid pipeline substantially outperforms general-purpose language models" and Section 8: "Moving from synthetic corpora to de-identified real-world notes is the central empirical risk." A position paper does not need new experiments, but if it leans on a companion study it should report the task, corpus size and provenance (synthetic, by whom), the baselines and how they were prompted, and the headline numbers with the metric the paper itself proposes. "Substantially outperforms" against un-tuned generative baselines on a synthetic corpus, in a paper arguing that executable correctness is the right metric, is exactly the kind of claim reviewers will discount. Either report the numbers and the caveats, or reframe the study as an illustration and stop calling it "evidence that the executable target is tractable."

**W6. The worked example has a clinical-realism error and quietly assumes away the paper's own hardest sub-problems.** "Discharge summary, authored by the primary-care physician (PCP)": discharge summaries are authored by the discharging inpatient team; the PCP is the recipient. This matters because *who orders the repeat CBC* (hospitalist places a future order vs. PCP is expected to) is the actor-disambiguation problem the paper names in 5.3, and the example resolves it by fiat ("actor: PCP"). Similarly the "+14 d, relative, encounter-anchored" constraint does not say which encounter (admission? discharge? dictation?), the escalation threshold "day 21" appears nowhere in the ACR schema, and the condition "palpitations recur" is patient-observable only, so the "conditional ServiceRequest gated on a documented recurrence" has no observer. The example should be corrected and should *show* the ambiguity (e.g., actor unresolved with a confidence entry and readiness rung "actionable, not executable") rather than hide it.

**W7. Causal overreach in the motivation.** Section 1: "These are not failures of clinical knowledge but of an intended action never becoming machine-actionable." The cited literature [3-9] attributes loop-closure failures to scheduling, access, insurance, patient factors, and inter-provider communication, not primarily to non-computable intent. Moreover, Singh and colleagues showed that follow-up still fails at meaningful rates even when abnormal results are delivered as structured EHR alerts (e.g., Singh H et al., Arch Intern Med 2009), which is evidence that structuring alone does not close loops. The paper concedes loop closure is "downstream" (Sec. 7) but the introduction claims the opposite. The manuscript also never quantifies *how much* intent is free-text-only; the "tests pending at discharge" and radiology-recommendation-not-ordered literatures (Roy CL et al. 2005; Were MC et al. 2009; Dalal AK et al.) exist precisely to size this and should be used.

**W8. The framework's internal logic breaks at layer 3, and the "generation" terminology is a dangling artifact.** Section 2 defines the five markers, one of which is "a *residual limitation* that motivates the next layer's object." Table 1 gives layer 3's residual limitation as "Safe, reliable execution," which motivates no next unit and is just the layer's own unsolved engineering. Either drop that row for layer 3 or propose what the next unit would be. Section 1 says "We use *generation* for a new computational layer," yet the word "generation" is never used again in that sense (only "layer"). Delete the sentence or use the term. More broadly, the two retrospective layers are fitted to markers defined in the same paper, so the framework has no demonstrated discriminative power; consider testing it against a candidate that should *fail* (e.g., cloud hosting, or predictive risk modeling) to show it excludes something.

**W9. AI/methods depth is below AIIM's bar.** Section 6 is two paragraphs. It cites the neuro-symbolic survey [42] and one application [43] but does not engage with constrained/grammar-guided decoding or structured-output generation, selective prediction (Geifman & El-Yaniv) and calibration in clinical NLP, argument-role extraction architectures, or how the "invariants" would be enforced (post-hoc validators? typed intermediate representations? the deterministic temporal component's failure mode when anchors are absent?). "Deterministic reasoning resolves times and due dates [45-48]" is an overstatement; HeidelTime and SUTime resolve *expressions*, not anchors, and "before the next visit" is not deterministic without scheduling data. Regulatory classification (FDA CDS guidance 2022 criteria; EU AI Act high-risk category) is listed as a future-work bullet, but a system that drafts orders from ambient audio sits on that boundary and an AIIM position paper should analyze it, not defer it.

## 5. Minor issues / line-by-line notes

- **Abstract vs. Introduction:** the first two sentences of the abstract and of Section 1 are near-verbatim duplicates. Vary one.
- **Abstract:** 245 words, within the 250 limit but with no margin; any revision must be re-counted.
- **Sec. 1, "[1,2]"** cited for "the unit of information it makes computable": refs 1 and 2 are EHR-history reviews; neither proposes that organizing principle. Cite them for the history, not the principle.
- **Sec. 2:** "differ in epistemic status" is good, but Figure 1's inner labels ("available / interpretable / operational by computers") introduce a third ladder never mentioned in the text. Either explain it or remove it.
- **Table 1, "Enabling technology":** "symbolic reasoning" also characterizes layer 2 (terminologies, rule-based CDS, cTAKES); the row does not discriminate layers 2 and 3.
- **Sec. 3:** "EMRAM [11,12]": ref 11 is a single Beijing Stage-7 hospital cross-sectional study; cite HIMSS EMRAM itself or a review.
- **Sec. 4:** "computer-interpretable guidelines and care-pathway models encode executable protocols [24-26]": ref 25 (Lenz & Reichert) is about process-aware IS, not CIGs; ref 26 is pathway modelling. Fine, but the sentence conflates three literatures.
- **Sec. 4, ref [55]:** Griffin et al. 2022 is a scoping review of *published* FHIR apps; it is evidence about the research literature, not about production EHR implementation. Soften "far less commonly implemented" to "far less commonly reported."
- **Sec. 5, terminology paragraph:** "a set of ACRs and related events is a clinical *workflow*" conflates a plan with its enactment; and "care process" as "the enacted sequence over time" collides with Table 2's "observed" category. Tighten.
- **Definition 1:** "*confidence* is a per-attribute map ... for inferred attributes," yet the example gives `confidence: temporal 0.86` for a temporal constraint that was *communicated* verbatim ("in two weeks"). Either confidence applies to all attributes or the example is inconsistent with the definition. ACR 2 has no confidence at all.
- **Definition 1 / Fig. 2:** the schema has no identifier, version, or `supersedes` link, yet Section 8 lists reconciliation/revocation as a core problem. At minimum an `id` and a `supersedes` attribute belong in the tuple.
- **Readiness ladder:** "interpreted" is never defined; the example says extracting "repeat complete blood count" is "partial" without naming the rung.
- **Worked example, "LOINC 58410-2":** correct code (CBC panel, automated count). Fine.
- **Worked example, "Mapping":** "a conditional ServiceRequest gated on a documented recurrence": name the element (`asNeeded[x]`) or the PlanDefinition action condition you would use; as written it reads as if FHIR lacks this.
- **Sec. 5.2, Fig. 3:** the central box says "recovery + structured reasoning" but the figure gives no indication of the human-confirmation step that Section 6 makes mandatory. Add the review loop to the figure.
- **Sec. 5.2:** "moving ambient AI from note generation toward order generation" is presented as a future direction; ambient-scribe vendors have been shipping draft order suggestions since 2024-2025. Acknowledge and cite, then say what the ACR adds (auditability, invariants, evaluation).
- **Sec. 5.3:** "These are not solved by a larger generator; they are structural requirements of the output." Assertion; either argue it or cut it (Section 6 makes the point better).
- **Sec. 6:** "[35,39]" for LLM clinical capability: ref 39 (GPT-4 on USMLE, 2023) is dated for a 2026 submission; replace with a 2025-2026 clinical IE evaluation.
- **Sec. 6, "[41]" (TimeBench 2024):** benchmark results on 2023-2024 models are weak support for "known to be weak" in 2026; add a more recent source or hedge to the specific failure mode (anchor resolution).
- **Sec. 7:** "with the framework's value resting on its constructs" is circular; the sentence says the framework is valuable because of what it contains.
- **Sec. 8, predictions:** "machine-actionable use of FHIR workflow resources should grow relative to free-text follow-up" would happen with or without this paper's thesis and does not test it. Keep only predictions that discriminate the thesis.
- **Sec. 8:** "distinguishing patient from clinician intent" is a core sub-problem (patient self-management commitments vs. orders) but the schema has no attribute for intent origin/type. Add one or explain why `actor` + `provenance.speaker` suffices.
- **Ethics:** no discussion of consent for ambient transcripts, or of the patient-facing consequences of a wrongly recovered conditional referral. One paragraph is warranted.
- **Ref 49:** listed as *Pac Symp Biocomput* 2026 but the DOI (10.1101/...) is a medRxiv preprint DOI. Reconcile.
- **Refs 29, 51:** arXiv items lack a URL/DOI form; AIIM asks for `https://doi.org/...` style. Ref 51 (2026 preprint, authored by the present authors' group) should be flagged as a preprint in text.
- **References style:** the journal example uses `https://doi.org/`; the manuscript uses `doi:`. Cosmetic but easy.
- **Highlights file** (3-5 bullets, <= 85 chars) is required by the journal and not present in the submission materials I was given.
- **Fig. 2** is essentially a restatement of Definition 1 as boxes; consider replacing it with something that carries information the text does not (e.g., the readiness ladder with example ACRs at each rung).

## 6. Fit to AIIM and the Position-paper type

Scope is right: recovering executable structure from clinical language with a hybrid architecture and a safety contract is a core AI-in-medicine topic, and AIIM readers care about exactly the evaluation question the paper raises. The problem is depth. As written, roughly half the body (Sections 2-4) is health-IT periodization and standards positioning, while the AI content (5.3, 6, 7) totals about six paragraphs. The three-layer framework is the least novel and least AI-relevant part of the manuscript and currently gets the most space; the challenge analysis, architecture, and evaluation, which are the AIIM contribution, get the least. The paper reads more like a JAMIA/JBI perspective on health IT than an AIIM position paper. The literature grounding is good on the health-services side and thin on the NLP/AI side (no dialogue-NLU intent frames, no executable semantic parsing, no constrained decoding, no selective prediction, no process-model-extraction-from-text). Rebalancing toward Sections 5.3-7, with the framework compressed to a page, would fix the fit.

## 7. Questions for the authors

- **Q1.** What does an ACR carry that a FHIR R5 `ServiceRequest` with `intent=proposal`, `status=draft`, `asNeeded[x]`, `occurrence[x]`, `performer`, plus `Provenance` and an extension for confidence, cannot? Please answer attribute by attribute.
- **Q2.** In the companion study [51]: what is the corpus (size, synthetic generation method), what are the baselines and how were they prompted or tuned, and what are the numbers on action-time pairing under the metrics this paper proposes?
- **Q3.** How are predicted ACRs aligned to gold ACRs before record-level metrics are computed, and how is "operationally unsafe output" defined for the Section 8 falsification test?
- **Q4.** What fraction of patient-specific intended actions in a typical discharge summary or ambulatory note is *not* also present as a structured future order in a modern CPOE-equipped EHR? Is there evidence, or is this an assumption?
- **Q5.** For "in two weeks" in a discharge summary, which anchor does the deterministic component use, and what happens when the anchor is absent ("before the next visit" with no scheduled visit)? Is that an abstention, an unresolved constraint at the "actionable" rung, or an error?
- **Q6.** How do two ACRs from different messages get recognized as the same intent for reconciliation, given the schema has no identifier or supersedes link?
- **Q7.** Who observes the condition "palpitations recur"? If it is patient-reported, does the system need a patient-facing channel, and does that change the actor and the regulatory posture?
- **Q8.** Why is the framework of five markers to be believed as discriminative? Is there a candidate technology it correctly excludes?

## 8. Prioritized improvement plan

1. **Rewrite Section 5.3 as the paper's core** (one subsection per sub-problem, state of the art with results, why the clinical-intent instance is harder, what "solved" means, 3-6 references each). This is the single change that turns the manuscript into an AIIM position paper.
2. **Add an ACR-to-FHIR R5 attribute mapping table** that names elements (`intent`, `asNeeded[x]`, `occurrence[x]`, `restriction.period`, `basedOn`/`replaces`, `Provenance`) and states per row what is lossless and what is not; then narrow the novelty claim to the demonstrated residue.
3. **Add a feature-by-feature comparison table** against CLIP, MedDec, MEDIQA-OE, radiology-recommendation extraction, and i2b2/n2c2 temporal tasks, and connect the ACR to dialogue-NLU intent frames and to executable semantic parsing (execution accuracy) as antecedents of "executable correctness."
4. **Specify the evaluation framework formally**: alignment procedure, metric definitions with units and tolerances, treatment of unpopulated optional attributes, consequence weighting, the selective-risk curve, a definition of "operationally unsafe," and a worked scoring of the paper's own example against a perturbed prediction.
5. **Report the companion study properly** (corpus, baselines, numbers, caveats about synthetic data and preprint status) or downgrade its role from "evidence of tractability" to "illustration."
6. **Fix the worked example**: correct authorship of the discharge summary, make the actor ambiguity and the anchor explicit, show a confidence map consistent with Definition 1, place the escalation window somewhere in the schema or the mapping, and name the FHIR element used for the conditional.
7. **Correct the motivation's causal claim** and size the gap with the tests-pending-at-discharge and recommendation-not-ordered literatures; acknowledge evidence that structured alerts alone do not close loops.
8. **Compress Sections 2-4 to about one page**, fix the layer-3 "residual limitation" inconsistency, remove the orphan "generation" sentence, add a short regulatory/ethics paragraph, expand Section 6 with concrete enforcement mechanisms for the invariants, and add the missing Highlights file.
